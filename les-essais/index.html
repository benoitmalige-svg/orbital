03 / THE FACULTY OF JUDGMENT

Having an answer doesn't mean you've thought it through.

Think about how much of what you believe was developed through your own reasoning. How many opinions have you adopted because someone expressed them convincingly? How often have you felt certain about something until you encountered an equally persuasive argument for the opposite position?

We’ve never had more answers available to us. As independent judgment becomes rarer, the ability to think for yourself becomes more valuable.

The quality of your judgment shapes which opportunities you recognize, which risks you take, whom you trust, and ultimately the life you build. It’s an advantage worth deliberately developing.

04 / THE MONTHLY EXPERIMENT

See what others don't.

The most valuable thinking practice I've discovered is spending time with a single question. The longer I explore it, the more assumptions I uncover, connections I notice, and possibilities I begin to see that weren't visible before.

And the advantage extends far beyond the question itself. You begin recognizing patterns across seemingly unrelated subjects, connecting ideas faster, and approaching decisions with perspectives you wouldn't have considered before.

Every month, we take one question and spend thirty days exploring it. The printed essay gives you a starting point. Your own experiences, observations, and reasoning take the investigation further.

I'm doing the same work alongside you. We may arrive at different conclusions. That's precisely what makes the exchange valuable.

05 / THE ORIGIN · 1580

Nearly 450 years later, we’re still studying what one man wrote to understand himself.

In 1580, Michel de Montaigne published his Essais. The word means attempts. He wrote to examine his experiences, beliefs, contradictions, and uncertainties, revising his work as his thinking evolved.

Centuries later, those personal investigations remain among the most influential works in philosophical literature.

Imagine what you might discover by taking your own thinking that seriously.

Les Essais brings that practice into the present. Every month, we take a question worth investigating and see where it leads.

objections.

01Wait. You’re sending me actual paper?
Yes. Actual paper, actual ink, an actual envelope, delivered to your actual home. A mildly unreasonable undertaking in 2026, but that’s part of the appeal.
02What am I actually supposed to do with it?
This is a co-authored investigation: I begin the correspondence, and you continue it. Each month, we explore the same question for 30 days through our own writing, observations, experiences, and conversations. I’m doing the work alongside you. The pages are three-hole-punched, so you can keep them in a binder, add your own notes, create folders, or organize your thinking however you like. Over time, you build a physical record of how your judgment develops.
03Why wouldn’t you just email it to me?
Because an email is very easy to open, skim, and forget. I want this to be something you can sit with, underline, disagree with, and return to. Something that doesn’t disappear beneath tomorrow’s notifications.
04What does my membership include?

A new printed essay each full membership month, access to The Symposium, and monthly live discussions. The $15 introduction includes The Identity Tax, the current digital issue, and immediate Symposium access. Your first printed edition follows if you continue into the first full month.

05What happens in The Symposium?
It’s our private space to continue the investigation together. Share an argument, ask a question, challenge an assumption, or see how someone else approaches the same problem. You’ll meet people who think like you, and hopefully plenty who don’t. The aim is to expand our thinking and develop better judgment. Once a month, we also have a live, open discussion with me. You can participate as much or as little as you want.
06Why practice independent judgment?

Anyone can generate a convincing answer. Knowing whether it is any good takes judgment. That cannot be downloaded or convincingly faked when a real decision is on the line. It is earned through years of questioning, observing, deciding, and learning. The better you get at it, the more valuable you become.
